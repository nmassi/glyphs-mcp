# Typeface DNA Framework

Use this framework when the user wants to change a typeface's DNA, voice, or differentiation without relying on vague stylistic language.

## Core idea

A typeface's identity is a system, not a single parameter. Divide it into structural, modulating, and textural variables. A superficial change rarely creates a new identity while the underlying construction remains untouched.

## Structural variables

### Skeleton and construction

The drawing logic behind `n`, `o`, `a`, `g`, `R`, `Q`, and related forms. It controls family resemblance and the downstream system of counters, joins, spacing, and interpolation.

Inspect `H O n o a g R Q S 2 4 7 &`, including path logic and node placement.

### Proportion and width system

Relative widths and heights determine whether a family feels compact, expansive, formal, friendly, static, or lively. Compare `H/O`, `n/o`, `R/H`, `S/H`, figure widths, x-height, cap height, ascenders, and descenders.

### Stress and axis

The direction of weight in curves can feel constructed, transitional, or calligraphic. Inspect its effect on `O`, `o`, `e`, `c`, and `S`, not as an isolated angle.

## Modulating variables

### Weight and contrast

Weight controls presence and darkness. Contrast controls the relation between thick and thin zones. Neither transfers safely between designs without recalibrating counters, spacing, stress, and small-size behavior.

### Tension

Tension is an inferred system produced by handle distribution, stress, counter pressure, aperture, and the rate at which curves accelerate into straights. It affects whether forms feel relaxed, neutral, or dramatic.

### Counters and apertures

Counters regulate density and internal pressure. Apertures in `c`, `e`, `a`, and `s` influence openness, legibility, and personality. Evaluate them at target sizes and in words.

### Terminals and joins

Stroke endings and junctions can shift a family from hard to soft or industrial to humanist. They must form a repeatable system rather than a collection of decorative exceptions.

## Textural variables

### Spacing and rhythm

Sidebearings create the recurring pattern of black and white. Rhythm determines whether lines feel stable, stiff, lively, or uneven. Diagnose texture before redrawing forms unnecessarily.

### Color

Color is the total gray value of text and emerges from outline mass, counters, spacing, word space, and line spacing. It is not an independent knob.

### Kerning behavior

Kerning corrects exceptional relationships after spacing. A family with excessive exceptions usually has unresolved spacing or grouping logic.

## Differentiation protocol

1. Resolve `H O n o` to establish proportions, stems, rounds, counters, and spacing.
2. Use `O/o` to define stress, contrast, counter pressure, and curve tension.
3. Use `S a g Q R 2 4 7 &` to establish distinctive construction and details.
4. Validate spacing, rhythm, and color in text.
5. Refine terminals, joins, traps, and local details only after the system works.

Choose the primary lever from the diagnosis:

- Too derivative structurally: change skeleton or proportions first.
- Similar flavor but distinct structure: adjust contrast, tension, apertures, and terminals.
- Weak text texture: fix spacing, color, and rhythm before widespread redrawing.

## Borrowing from references

When a reference contains an attractive trait:

1. Measure and describe the trait as a principle.
2. Identify the variables supporting it.
3. Freeze the receiving family's invariants.
4. Import the principle in one controlled pass.
5. Recheck counters, color, spacing, compatibility, and interpolation.

Do not assume that contrast, tension, width, or another trait can move in isolation. If structure, texture, and distinctive glyphs still identify one donor immediately, the result needs further differentiation.

## Evidence map

| Variable | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Skeleton | Outline construction and related glyph logic |
| Proportion | Width and height ratios across representative forms |
| Weight | Straight, round, diagonal, and join measurements plus visual proof |
| Color | Glyph density and repeated text strings |
| Spacing | Sidebearings, edge groups, words, and paragraphs |
| Interpolation | Compatible masters and representative intermediate instances |

Use measurements to make design moves explainable. Use visual judgment to decide whether those moves work.
